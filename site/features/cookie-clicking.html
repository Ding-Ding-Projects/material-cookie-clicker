Cookie clicking and the CPS loop

The primary click target, click multipliers, the cookies-per-second accumulator, and the offline-progress rules that make a closed window honest.

Current state

What it does

Clicking the cookie adds a base click value of 1, multiplied by every active click multiplier from upgrades, tools and prestige, to your cookie total and to your lifetime total. Each click also increments a total-click counter, which is itself an unlock condition for several achievements and tools.

Independently of clicking, every generator you own contributes a fixed cookies-per-second rate. Total CPS is derived, never stored: it is recomputed from owned generators, their per-generator multipliers and the global multiplier every time it is asked for, so it can never drift out of step with what you own.

Production over a period of time is a pure function of an elapsed-milliseconds delta the caller supplies. The engine never reads a clock itself and never assumes a frame rate, which is what makes the loop testable and what stops a throttled background window from quietly losing cookies.

Offline progress

When the game reopens, cookies earned while it was closed are computed from the last applied tick timestamp to now, at a configurable fraction of normal CPS and capped at a configurable maximum offline duration. The clock rules are deliberate and worth stating plainly:

  • A negative elapsed time — the clock moved backwards, or a save file was hand-edited into the future — counts as zero elapsed time and is recorded as a clock anomaly. The absolute value is never taken, because that would turn winding your clock back into free cookies.
  • An absurdly large forward jump is clamped to the cap rather than flagged; a real machine can plausibly be off for a long time.
  • A malformed timestamp never throws. Throwing here would brick loading a save, so anomalies are always reported through the result value instead.

How it is configured

The offline cap and the offline CPS fraction are options passed in by the caller, not constants baked into the loop. A separate wrapper widens those options with any active offline bonuses from the Tools tech tree before computing, so the core function keeps no hidden dependency on tool state.

The design/cookie-surface.html spec fixes the presentation the screen must honour once it exists: rest, hover, pressed, focus-visible, reduced-motion and disabled states on a 168px arcade-button cookie, a floating “+N” popup that respects reduced motion, and a disabled state that carries a reason a screen reader can announce rather than a silently dead button.

In the shipped build

The cookie is on screen and clickable, with a hold-to-click-repeatedly mode, and the three HUD readouts above it (cookies, per second, per click) update live. What has not been photographed is the dark theme, and the narrow-width layout was checked by forcing its breakpoint rather than by really resizing the window.
