The diesel factory

Cookies buy the plant. The plant makes the diesel. A four-stage production line that stalls honestly when a stage cannot run, and ships real litres to a second application.

Current state

Shipped the Diesel Factory is its own console panel in this release; the economy is in src/shared/game/diesel-factory.ts.

What changed, and why

An earlier build sold diesel outright. You pressed a button, a thousand cookies vanished, and a litre existed. Nothing made it — the litre came out of thin air, and the price curve was the only thing standing between a player and an unlimited supply of fuel for a nuclear plant simulator.

That is gone. Diesel is manufactured now, by a small refinery you build and run, and cookies touch it in exactly one place: buying equipment. There is no cookies-to-litres exchange rate anywhere in the game any more.

From the running application

The line, stage by stage

Wells and importers pull crude out of the ground. Refining units convert crude into litres, at a throughput and at an efficiency. Storage tanks hold the litres and are finite. The depot ships litres out as vouchers, drawing the tanks down.

Every arrow in that chain can stall, and stalls honestly:

  • A refinery with no crude in the yard refines nothing.
  • A refinery with a full tank in front of it refines nothing either, and the crude it would have used stays in the yard rather than evaporating.
  • Wells pumping into a full yard stop pumping.
  • The depot cannot ship a litre the tanks do not hold. The button is dead and says why.

The panel shows which stage stopped by dimming it and naming the reason, and it separates the rate the plant is rated for from the rate it is currently achieving — two readouts, never one.

The rate curve

Crude Well2,000×1.15+0.05 barrels/sec

Refinery Still8,000×1.15+0.02 litres/sec of throughput
Catalytic Cracker120,000×1.15+0.2 litres/sec
Storage Tank5,000×1.15+25 litres of tank, +50 barrels of yard
Transfer Pump40,000×1.15+8% throughput on every refining unit

Every line grows 1.15 a unit — the same house ratio the generator ladder uses, so the factory shop reads without being taught anything new. Conversion starts at 2.5 barrels per litre; storage starts at a 10-litre drum and a 20-barrel hardstanding.

Those numbers teach the mechanic by themselves. One well feeds exactly one still — 0.05 barrels a second produced against 0.02 × 2.5 = 0.05 demanded — and one importer feeds exactly one cracker. Buy wells alone and the yard backs up; buy stills alone and they idle. A fresh floor also runs out of yard before it runs out of drum: twenty barrels is eight litres and the drum holds ten, which is why the first Storage Tank raises both numbers at once.

Fourteen factory upgrades, in four branches

  • Throughput — Wider Bore, Deep Drilling and Pipeline Spur on the intake side; Hot Feed and Continuous Run on the refining side.
  • Efficiency — Trayed Column, Vacuum Distillation and Hydrocracking cut the barrels each litre costs. The only branch that makes an existing line cheaper to run rather than bigger.
  • Capacity — Bunded Bay, Floating Roof and Tank Farm raise the ceiling the line stalls against.
  • Automation — Depot Telemetry ships at a full tank, Dispatch Desk at half, Night Shift at a quarter. Automation is bought and then switched on; buying it never starts it.

Nothing unlocks itself. An upgrade whose condition is not met is absent rather than dimmed, the same rule the generator ladder follows, so a locked list never becomes a list of spoilers. The factory itself arrives with the Fuel Contract upgrade — signing a contract to supply WinForge is what gives you a reason to build a refinery — and that purchase grants a surface, never a piece of equipment. The floor starts bare.

Shipping to WinForge

Shipping is a withdrawal, not a purchase. It writes one voucher into a small JSON file that a separate application, WinForge, reads to fuel its reactor simulator's emergency diesel generators. The whole contract is documented in the repository, and the file format has not changed.

One thing about that file has been clarified. A voucher carries a cookiesSpent figure, and nobody pays cookies at the shipping counter any more, so the number is now the shipment's amortized share of what the plant cost to build: cookies invested, times this shipment's litres over every litre the plant has ever made. The field is still a string, still a receipt, and the schema version is still 1 — a reader written against the old format needs no change at all.

In the shipped build

The factory is a console emblem and an anchored dialog, bilingual throughout, with the production floor, the equipment shop, the upgrade tree and the shipping station all on one scrolling surface. The Diesel Depot card in the shop rail's footer became a compact status light with a tank gauge and a door into the panel — there is one Ship button in the game, not two.

What is not photographed here: the reduced-motion rendering of this panel, where the pipe flow and the derrick stop, which is declared in CSS and asserted nowhere else. That is a gap in evidence, not a feature known to be broken. The automation branch used to be listed beside it; the capture above closes that one.
