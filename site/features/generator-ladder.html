The 20-tier generator ladder

Cursor up to the Wok of the Gods: twenty generator tiers, a constant 1.15 cost curve, and the buy-quantity stepper.

Current state

Shipped the shop rail is in this release; 20 definitions in src/shared/game/generators.ts.

From the running application

See all the captures in the capture matrix.

What it does

Twenty generator tiers bake cookies automatically once owned. Each tier's base cost and base output are roughly ten times the previous tier's, so the “the next tier is finally worth it” moment recurs at a predictable cadence for the whole run. Every tier carries an English name and a Hong Kong Cantonese name, and the Cantonese names lean into local jokes rather than literal translation.

Every tier uses the same per-unit cost growth ratio of 1.15, held constant across the whole ladder rather than tuned per tier, so the price of the next unit is a single predictable formula the player can reason about.

How it is configured

Cost, output and growth ratio are data in one file, not scattered through the purchase logic. A row's three presentation states are fixed by design/building-row.html: affordable, unaffordable (visible but disabled, never hidden), and locked (shown-and-locked, so you always know what is coming). The buy-quantity stepper offers ×1, ×10, ×100 and Max as a segmented cabinet switch.

The rule about locks

Locking a later tier behind an earlier one's progress is a pacing decision for the game. It gates a purchase, and only a purchase. It is unrelated to the Tools tech tree, which never gates an application feature.

In the shipped build

The shop rail is docked beside the cookie on the same screen, with a search field, per-row x1/x10/x100/Max steppers and Buy buttons. Two caveats worth stating: an unlock toast can briefly overlay a shop row, which is why the capture above was taken only once the toast queue had drained; and the narrow-width behaviour where the rail becomes a bottom-sheet drawer was verified by forcing the breakpoint, not by a real window resize. The rail also holds the Diesel Depot as a docked footer, which is what leaves the ladder above it a scrolling column — and, as the second capture shows, what keeps that column to about one row of visible height.
