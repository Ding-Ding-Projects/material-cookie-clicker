Upgrades

A hundred and eighty upgrades: click lines, global lines, a golden-cookie line, the kitten line that drinks your milk, five stacking tiers per generator, and a synergy in both directions for every neighbouring pair.

Current state

Shipped the upgrade ticket strip is in this release; 79 definitions in src/shared/game/upgrades.ts.

What it does

An upgrade is a one-time purchase that permanently multiplies something. There are 180 of them, and that number is not a target — it is what the definition list actually contains:

  • 5 reveal upgrades — Shop Sign (10 cookies), Upgrade Catalogue (50), Steady Hand (100), Fuel Contract (500) and the Property Deed (2,000). These multiply nothing at all. A fresh save is the cookie and its counter alone, and each of these buys back one piece of the game’s own surface: the shop rail, this very ticket strip, press-and-hold clicking, and the whole diesel factory — its console panel and the depot status card in the shop rail’s footer together — and the bakery-home, which arrives as an empty plot with nothing built on it. Before the strip exists there is nowhere to buy them from, so the next one appears as a single discovery ticket beside the cookie once its price is within reach. None of the five is ever handed out: a save carried forward from an older version is not granted them either, and in particular Steady Hand — which changes what holding the mouse button does — only ever arrives by purchase.
  • 10 click upgrades — three unlocked by lifetime cookies, seven by how much you have actually clicked, from a Callused Knuckle up to the Finger of the Ancestor.
  • 10 global upgrades, unlocked by lifetime-cookie thresholds, from Sturdier Ovens up to The Recipe That Bends Physics.
  • 7 golden-cookie upgrades — a chain that makes a caught golden cookie pay more and shortens the wait a catch schedules. They change no production number at all.
  • 10 kitten upgrades — the only things in the game that read milk. A kitten converts the milk level (4% per unlocked achievement) into a multiplier on total production, so its value climbs every time a badge is struck without the player buying anything else. Each one unlocks on an achievement count rather than a cookie count, because a kitten bought with an empty cabinet would be an expensive ×1.
  • 38 synergy upgrades — one in each direction for every neighbouring pair on the ladder. A synergy is bought once and then keeps paying by itself: the Temple line is worth more for every Bank you own, and the Bank line for every Temple. The lift is read out of live state, so buying one more machine immediately makes its neighbour worth more.
  • 100 per-generator upgrades — five tiers for each of the twenty generators, unlocking at 1, 5, 25, 50 and 100 owned. Each tier doubles that generator's own output, so all five stacked reach a 32× multiplier on that tier at 100 owned.

Seven effect kinds exist and no more: a click multiplier, a multiplier on one named generator, a global CPS multiplier, a synergy, a kitten, a golden-cookie improvement, and a reveal — which turns on a piece of the game surface and changes no number anywhere. Unlock conditions are equally small: an owned count of a named generator, a lifetime-cookie threshold, a click count, an achievement count, another upgrade already owned, or always available. A reveal gates game panels and one game input behaviour only; it is never allowed to gate a real application feature, which the Tools tech tree’s own contract keeps unconditionally available.

How it is configured

The thresholds and the per-tier multiplier are named constants at the top of the file, and the hundred per-generator upgrades and the thirty-eight synergies are generated from them rather than typed out, so a change to the ladder cannot leave a stale upgrade behind. Prices for the generated tiers derive from the generator's own base cost.

Presentation is fixed by design/upgrade-card.html: upgrades are ticket-shaped purchases with notches punched out of both edges, a dashed perforation and a price stub, in locked, unlocked-buyable and owned states, with a metallic treatment once owned and a hover tilt that has a still equivalent under reduced motion.

In the shipped build

The upgrade shelf sits on the same screen as the cookie and the shop, with its own search field and a live “owned / 180” counter. It is no longer one horizontal strip: at a hundred and eighty upgrades the handful you can actually buy were lost in the middle of a kilometre of ones you already owned. The shelf now says three different things in three different shapes — a wrapping grid of buyable tickets sorted next-affordable-first and capped at two visible rows, a short list of the locked upgrades closest to unlocking shown as requirement lines rather than prices, and the ones you already bought collapsed into a compact stamp wall with a count badge. The strip is not on screen at the start of a run: it arrives with the Upgrade Catalogue. Nothing here is ever handed out on load — the version-2 to version-3 migration deliberately grants nothing, and a save carried forward keeps the surfaces it demonstrably used by derivation from its own progress instead, by deriving them from the progress already in the save. The dark-theme capture on the front page shows the same shelf rendered from the dark token set.

These 180 are the game’s own upgrades. The diesel factory keeps a second, separate shelf of its own — 6 pieces of equipment and 14 factory upgrades, bought with cookies inside the factory panel — and those are not counted in the 179 or in the shelf’s owned counter.
